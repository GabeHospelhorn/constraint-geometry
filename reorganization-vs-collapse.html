Reorganization vs Collapse

Π_norm · ΔΦ · E

A natural objection: parallax flags that a system is leaving its current basin, but reorganization and collapse both involve change. Does the signal confuse a system coming apart with one reorganizing into something better? This module reuses the monitor's estimators and shows the two cases have different signatures.

Collapse is coherent decline: frames fall together, so parallax stays low while the margin erodes. Reorganization is a parallax excursion that resolves: frames disagree transiently, then re-cohere, and the contradiction burden settles back down rather than climbing.

the observable difference

Two separations do the work. First, coherence: collapse declines coherently, so its parallax stays near baseline, while reorganization produces a genuine parallax excursion with low sign coherence. Second, for any run that does show a parallax excursion, the direction of the contradiction burden after the peak separates adaptation from pathology.

sign(dE/dt) after the parallax peak:
  dE/dt > 0  (burden still rising) → pathology / collapse signature
  dE/dt ≤ 0  (burden falling / settling) → reorganization signature

This is partly retrospective by necessity: confirming reorganization requires watching it resolve. The monitor still earns its keep: it flags the regime that warrants attention, and the burden trajectory tells you which kind it was, often well before behavior would have revealed it.

Scope. Constructive simulation under specified parametric dynamics, not validation on real model checkpoints. It demonstrates that the discriminator is present in the framework (collapse and reorganization differ in the sign of post-peak burden growth), not that any particular threshold transfers to a real system. The framework detects instability, leaving the current basin, not pathology, whether the destination is bad. Pathology is a downstream, partly normative judgment, which is why detection is kept separate from control.
